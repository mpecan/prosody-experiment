Prosody turns a poem into a piece of music. Not a backing track for the words: the poem itself is the score.

Each line becomes a bar. Stressed syllables become the beat. Rhymes become notes. Repeated lines become loops that build up and wear away. Rarer words sound brighter. A voice can read the poem over the music, and the words light up as they play.

Every sound traces back to a word. Change a word and you hear exactly what it changed, and nothing else in the piece moves. Tighten a line's metre and the rhythm tightens. Add a refrain and that instrument comes back.

WHAT YOU CAN DO
• Play 18 poems to start from: Blake, Dickinson, Carroll, Shakespeare, Poe, Hopkins, Whitman and more, one of them in Slovenian.
• Write your own, or paste one in, and press Play.
• Watch the music on a scrolling roll, layer by layer: pulse, ornament, bass, rhyme and chords.
• Hear it two ways: set how rhyme, stress and refrain are heard on A and on B, and switch between them while it plays.
• Choose a sound: piano, vibraphone, a jazz trio, lo-fi, techno, ambient, bossa, chamber, a hymn, or bare waves. The style changes how it sounds, never what happens.
• Mix the band and the voice, each layer on its own fader, with mute and solo.
• Have it read aloud: recorded readings of the example poems, your phone's own voices for anything you write, or neural voices you can download.
• Look under the hood: what each word became, and the pattern the poem compiled to.
• Share a poem as a link that opens to the same piece.
• Keep listening with the screen off, with play and stop on the lock screen.

PRIVATE AND OFFLINE
No account, no ads, no tracking. Your poems stay on your device. Everything works offline; the optional neural voices are downloaded only if you choose one, after you are asked.

AN EXPERIMENT
Prosody is an experiment in what a poem sounds like when its own patterns are the music. It is open source (MIT): how it works, and every rule it follows, is at github.com/mpecan/prosody-experiment.
