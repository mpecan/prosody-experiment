The poem decides where in the tempo range it sits, from how densely its syllables pack into bars. Everything else here is colour. Humanise strays each syllable a little late and soft, drawn from its own line, so the same poem always strays the same way.

What happens: which feature of the poem becomes which sound. Set A and B, then switch while it plays.

AB

Candidate mappings still under review. Each is a selector: choose a variant — or off — for each setting, then switch between A and B while it plays; the Score tab measures both. A bold border marks a choice that is not the default. The pulse has no off: steady gives every bar as long as its beats, elastic every bar the same span, as the page once played every poem. Nor has the stack: crossing keeps every line on its own feet, straight fits a line sounding under the bar's line to that line's subdivision wherever the two would cross.

A voice reading the poem over the music.

Whether and when lines are read is the reading mapping above: breath (the default) reads each stanza through, with a silent bar before the next stanza and never before the music has begun it; arrives speaks each line once, where it is first heard; stanza reads a stanza through from where it is first heard; off reads nothing. Loops, lanes and the outro are never read. At the natural pace a line follows the one before after a pause from how it ends — a quarter of a second after a line that runs on, more after a comma, more after a full stop, a second and a half at a stanza's end; fitted fits each line to its bar. Strudel's clock places every line; a neural voice is rendered ahead and started exactly on it, the browser's voice as near as its speech allows. Kokoro and Kitten speak English; Piper speaks many languages, Slovenian among them. Kitten needs WebGPU.

This is an experiment. Here a poem is the source code for a piece
      of music.

    
Each line becomes a bar. Stressed syllables become the beat. Rhymes become notes. Repeated lines
      become loops that build up and wear away. A voice can read the poem over it. Every sound
      traces back to a word, and the words light up as they play. Change a word and you'll hear
      exactly what it changed.

    
Pick a poem, or write your own, and press Play.
